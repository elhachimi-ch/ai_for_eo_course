Calibration: does 90 % mean 90 %?

Temperature scaling of the logits \(\mathbf{z}\): $$\hat{\mathbf{p}}=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{z}/T)$$ Expected calibration error over confidence bins \(B_m\): $$\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_m\frac{|B_m|}{n}\,\big|\operatorname{acc}(B_m)-\operatorname{conf}(B_m)\big|$$

Why EO needs it

Probabilities feed thresholds, area estimates, alerts and decisions. Deep networks trained with cross-entropy are usually over-confident; one scalar \(T\), fitted on held-out data, fixes much of it.

Left: reliability diagram. Blue bars are the accuracy in each confidence bin; the shaded part is the gap to the confidence the model claimed; the diagonal is perfect calibration. Right: how often each confidence occurs. The over-confident network puts most pixels near 100 % confidence but is right less often. Drag T above 1 (softer probabilities) or press “fit T”: ECE and NLL drop, accuracy does not change because the arg-max is the same. Calibration holds only for data like the validation set — after a domain shift it degrades again (two slides on).

Ensembles: aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty

Each member \(m\) predicts a mean \(\mu_m\) and a noise variance \(\sigma_m^2\): $$\sigma^2(\mathbf{x})=\underbrace{\tfrac1M\textstyle\sum_m\sigma_m^2}_{\text{aleatoric}}+\underbrace{\tfrac1M\textstyle\sum_m(\mu_m-\bar\mu)^2}_{\text{epistemic}}$$

Two kinds of “don't know”

Aleatoric: noise in the data (saturation, speckle) — more data won't remove it. Epistemic: the model has not seen such inputs — it grows in gaps and outside the training range.

Conformal prediction wraps any model: widen intervals by the \((1-\alpha)\) quantile of held-out residuals to guarantee coverage.
Biomass from L-band backscatter saturates and gets noisier at high biomass (heteroscedastic, aleatoric noise). Each ensemble member is trained on a bootstrap sample with different random features; where they agree the dark (epistemic) band is thin. Inside the gap and beyond the data (gold zone) they disagree — that disagreement is the warning. With M = 1 there is no epistemic estimate at all. MC dropout approximates the same idea with one network. Global canopy-height and biomass maps (e.g. Lang et al. 2023) now ship a per-pixel uncertainty layer built this way.

The readout also counts how many target points a Mahalanobis test flags as out-of-distribution — a model that knows when it is outside its training data can abstain instead of being confidently wrong.

Geographic bias: who is the model good for?

where the 600 training labels come from

The pattern

Most EO benchmarks and pre-training data come from Europe, North America and China. Accuracy is then lowest where maps are needed most — smallholder farms, drylands, informal settlements.

Report the worst region, not only the mean: $$\text{Acc}_{\min}=\min_{g}\ \text{Acc}_g$$ and train for it (group re-weighting, distributionally robust optimisation).
Kerner et al. (2024) found low agreement between existing cropland maps across Sub-Saharan Africa.
Six regions share the same three land-cover classes but differ in spectra: drier vegetation and brighter soils in North and Sub-Saharan Africa, haze and different crops elsewhere. With a typical benchmark mix (no African labels) the model is excellent in Europe and poor in Africa, although the mean accuracy looks acceptable. Move the slider: a modest share of local labels closes most of the gap at almost no cost elsewhere. The general lesson for every map you publish: report accuracy per region (or per city, per agro-ecological zone) and say where the model should not be used.

The carbon cost of training and inference

$$E=n_{\text{dev}}\cdot P\cdot t\cdot \text{PUE},\qquad \text{CO}_2\text{e}=E\cdot \text{CI}_{\text{grid}}$$
PUE: data-centre overhead · CI: carbon intensity of the electricity

Lighter options

  • Use embeddings or a frozen FM before full fine-tuning
  • Smaller models, mixed precision, early stopping
  • Run where and when the grid is clean
  • Report energy and CO₂e in the paper
Presets: fine-tuning a U-Net is a few kWh — negligible. Pre-training a foundation model on 64 A100s for two weeks is in the tonnes of CO₂e, comparable to several long-haul flights, and strongly dependent on the grid. Serving a model for months can exceed training. Numbers are orders of magnitude: real power draw is below the rated TDP, and embodied emissions from manufacturing the hardware are not counted. Zhu et al. (2026) list “carbon-minimised” as one of the 11 features of an ideal Earth FM.

Privacy, dual use and documentation

◉

Privacy

At 30–50 cm, houses, cars and gatherings are visible. Combined with other data, a map can identify people. Aggregate before publishing.

⚠

Dual use

The same detector counts ships for fisheries control or for military targeting; a settlement map guides upgrading — or evictions.

⇄

Communities

Involve the people being mapped: co-design, consent for field data, CARE principles for Indigenous data.

≡

Document

Datasheet for the labels, model card for the model (data, regions, limits, metrics per group), licence, FAIR release.

Before publishing a map, ask: who could be harmed by it, and who was not asked? Write the answer in the model card.
These four cards turn the lessons of the course into general rules. A model card is a one-page document: intended use, training data and regions, evaluation per group and region, known failure cases, ethical considerations. A datasheet does the same for a dataset: who collected the labels, when, how, with what consent and licence. Both take an afternoon and make a paper easier to review and reuse.
