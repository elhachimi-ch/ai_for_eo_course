The task

Only \(\{\mathbf{x}_i\}\), no \(y\). Find structure in \(p(\mathbf{x})\): groups (clustering), directions (dimensionality reduction), densities (mixtures, anomalies).

In remote sensing

Unsupervised classification (k-means / ISODATA, then an analyst names the clusters), PCA / MNF for multi- and hyperspectral data, PCA-based change detection.

Why it matters now

Labels are scarce, pixels are not. Self-supervised pre-training (Chapter V.6) is the modern heir: learn the structure of unlabelled archives first.

Unsupervised learning answers “what is in my data?” before anyone has labelled it. In EO it was the first practical form of machine learning: ISODATA clustering in the image-processing packages of the 1970s–90s, PCA for multispectral compression and change detection. Five demos follow: k-means step by step, k-means as an unsupervised land-cover map, Gaussian mixtures with EM, DBSCAN, and PCA.

k-means — assign, update, repeat

$$J=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big\lVert\mathbf{x}_i-\boldsymbol\mu_{c(i)}\big\rVert^2$$ $$\text{assign: } c(i)=\arg\min_k\lVert\mathbf{x}_i-\boldsymbol\mu_k\rVert^2\qquad \text{update: } \boldsymbol\mu_k=\frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{i\in C_k}\mathbf{x}_i$$

Coordinate descent on J

Each step can only lower \(J\), so the algorithm always converges — but to a local minimum that depends on the start. k-means++ picks each new centre with probability \(\propto D(\mathbf{x})^2\), the squared distance to the nearest centre already chosen.

k-means assumes round, equal-size clusters and you must choose \(k\). Try k = 2 and k = 7, or several random inits.
Demo: alternate Assign (pixels join the nearest centroid; Voronoi cells shaded) and Update (centroids jump to the mean of their pixels). Run iterates until no pixel changes. New init with random starts sometimes ends with two centroids sharing one group — a local minimum; k-means++ avoids most of those. The four groups are water, dense vegetation, sparse vegetation and soil in (Red, NIR) space.

The classic workflow

  1. stack bands, standardise
  2. cluster all pixels (k-means / ISODATA)
  3. an analyst labels each cluster
  4. merge or split clusters, check accuracy

Try it

Visible bands only: fields and roofs blur together. Add NIR and SWIR: purity against the true cover jumps — more spectral information, better clusters.

This is how many first land-cover maps were made: no training data, just clustering 25 600 pixels into k spectral groups, then naming them. The purity number compares each cluster with the true dominant cover of its pixels (which only we know, because the scene is synthetic). ISODATA adds rules to split clusters that are too spread out and merge those that are too close, so k adapts.

Gaussian mixtures — soft clusters, learned by EM

$$p(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}\mid\boldsymbol\mu_k,\Sigma_k)$$ $$\textbf{E: }\ \gamma_{ik}=\frac{\pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_i\mid\boldsymbol\mu_k,\Sigma_k)}{\sum_j\pi_j\,\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_i\mid\boldsymbol\mu_j,\Sigma_j)}\qquad \textbf{M: }\ N_k=\sum_i\gamma_{ik},\ \ \boldsymbol\mu_k=\frac{1}{N_k}\sum_i\gamma_{ik}\mathbf{x}_i$$
\(\Sigma_k=\frac{1}{N_k}\sum_i\gamma_{ik}(\mathbf{x}_i-\boldsymbol\mu_k)(\mathbf{x}_i-\boldsymbol\mu_k)^\top,\ \ \pi_k=N_k/n\)

EM in one sentence

Guess who belongs where (E), refit each Gaussian to its weighted pixels (M), repeat. The log-likelihood never decreases. k-means is the hard-assignment limit of EM with equal round covariances.

Soft memberships suit mixed pixels: a 10 m pixel can be 60 % crop and 40 % soil. The tilted ellipses are the supervised MLC's Gaussians — learned without labels.
Demo: start from a fresh init and alternate E and M. Point colours blend the cluster colours by their responsibilities γ, so uncertain pixels between clusters look mixed. Ellipses grow and tilt to follow the elongated vegetation and soil-line clouds, which k-means cannot do. Watch the log-likelihood climb and flatten.

DBSCAN — clusters of any shape, plus noise

$$N_\varepsilon(\mathbf{p})=\{\mathbf{q}:\lVert\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\rVert\le\varepsilon\},\qquad \mathbf{p}\ \text{is core}\iff|N_\varepsilon(\mathbf{p})|\ge\text{minPts}$$
A cluster = all points density-reachable from a core point · border points are reached but not core · the rest is noise

No k, no round shapes

Clusters grow through chains of dense neighbourhoods, so they follow rivers and roads. Isolated points stay noise (grey ×). Hover to see the ε-disc around the cursor.

EO uses: grouping building or ship detections, clustering fire hotspots into fire events, cleaning GNSS or LiDAR point clouds.
The points could be building detections from a model, or VIIRS fire hotspots: a settlement strung along a river, a second curved one, a compact village, and scattered false alarms. Switch to k-means (k = 3): it cuts the curved groups into round pieces and must assign every false alarm to some cluster. With DBSCAN, make ε too small and everything becomes noise; too large and the settlements merge. minPts sets how dense “dense” is.

PCA — the directions of greatest variance

$$\Sigma=\tfrac1n\sum_i(\mathbf{x}_i-\bar{\mathbf{x}})(\mathbf{x}_i-\bar{\mathbf{x}})^\top,\qquad \Sigma\,\mathbf{v}_k=\lambda_k\mathbf{v}_k,\qquad z_{ik}=\mathbf{v}_k^\top(\mathbf{x}_i-\bar{\mathbf{x}})$$
\(\mathbf{v}_1=\arg\max_{\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert=1}\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{x})\) · explained variance of PC \(k\) = \(\lambda_k/\sum_j\lambda_j\)

Lab 2 · Clusters, components and change — no labels

from sklearn.decomposition import PCA
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
from sklearn.mixture import GaussianMixture
from sklearn.metrics import adjusted_rand_score
from skimage.filters import threshold_otsu

Z = (X - X.mean(0)) / X.std(0)                 # valid pixels × 10 bands, standardised
pca = PCA(n_components=6).fit(Z)
print(pca.explained_variance_ratio_.cumsum())   # 3 PCs ≈ 98 % of the variance

km = KMeans(n_clusters=7, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit(Z[sample])
gmm = min((GaussianMixture(k, covariance_type="full", random_state=0).fit(Z[sample])
           for k in range(2, 13)), key=lambda g: g.bic(Z[sample]))
print("ARI vs WorldCover:", adjusted_rand_score(wc_flat[sample], km.labels_))

No model ever saw a label: the ARI against WorldCover is computed only after clustering — and it is low (≈ 0.1). Clusters are spectral, classes are thematic.
Same area and loader as Lab 1, so participants can compare supervised and unsupervised maps directly. PCA: the first component is overall brightness, the second separates vegetation (NIR) from soil and built-up (SWIR), the third picks up water and moisture — a reminder that PCA directions are statistical, not physical, but often interpretable. k-means finds compact groups of equal size and variance; a GMM with full covariances fits elongated spectral clouds better and gives a per-pixel probability. Change-vector analysis needs both dates on the same grid and comparable radiometry: standardising each date removes most of the illumination difference but also some real change — discuss. Comparing the same season avoids most phenological change, but not all: the clearest days fall in June, at the cereal harvest, so fields harvested a week apart flag as change — about 18 % of the Marrakech window is flagged, mostly field parcels, far more than real land-cover change. New construction on Marrakech's edges and new irrigated fields are among the true positives. Clusters versus WorldCover give a low ARI (about 0.1): bare soil, built-up and sparse grassland overlap spectrally in this semi-arid landscape, which is exactly why unsupervised maps need an analyst.
